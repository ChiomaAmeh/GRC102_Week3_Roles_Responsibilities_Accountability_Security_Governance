# Evidence Bundle 1: Governance Architecture
 
## Purpose
 
This evidence bundle evaluates TechGlobal's current security governance weaknesses and proposes a scalable governance architecture that aligns cybersecurity decision-making with business objectives, executive oversight, and Board accountability.
 
---
 
# 1. Governance Gap Assessment
 
## Current-State Governance Weaknesses
 
TechGlobal has experienced rapid growth but its security governance structure has not matured at the same pace. Security responsibilities are heavily concentrated within the IT Director role, resulting in governance, accountability, and risk management challenges.
 
### Governance Gap Assessment
 
| Current Weakness | Impact | Risk Level |
|-----------------|---------|------------|
| Security decisions concentrated within IT Director | Single point of authority and decision bottleneck | High |
| No formal security governance committees | Lack of coordinated decision making | High |
| Limited Board oversight of cyber risk | Strategic risks may remain unmanaged | High |
| Inconsistent business-unit security practices | Uneven security posture across the organisation | Medium |
| No formal cyber-risk escalation process | Delayed response to high-impact risks | High |
| Undefined accountability structure | Confusion over ownership and decision authority | High |
| Security viewed primarily as an IT issue | Weak business engagement in security governance | Medium |
 
---
 
## Governance Risks Identified
 
The current governance model creates the following challenges:
 
- Over-reliance on one individual for security decisions.
- Poor visibility of cyber risks at executive and Board levels.
- Lack of consistent governance across business units.
- Reduced accountability for risk ownership.
- Increased likelihood of regulatory and compliance failures.
- Delayed escalation of significant cyber events.

---
 
## Governance Design Principles
 
The proposed structure is based on:
 
1. Clear accountability.
2. Board-level oversight.
3. Executive ownership of cyber risk.
4. Separation of governance and operations.
5. Enterprise-wide participation.
6. Formal escalation pathways.
7. Risk-based decision-making.
 
---
 
# 4. Consultant Justification

The proposed governance architecture addresses the weaknesses identified within TechGlobal's existing operating model.
 
Board involvement ensures cybersecurity is treated as a strategic business risk rather than solely an IT issue. The introduction of a Security Governance Committee creates a formal mechanism for cross-functional decision-making and risk oversight.
 
The appointment of dedicated governance responsibilities for executive leadership ensures accountability is distributed appropriately across the organisation. This reduces dependence on the IT Director and promotes transparency, consistency, and compliance with industry governance practices.
 
Additionally, the structure improves communication between technical teams, business units, executive management, and the Board, enabling more effective oversight and timely escalation of significant cyber risks.
 
The governance model is scalable and capable of supporting TechGlobal's continued growth while improving organisational resilience and governance maturity.
 
---
 
# Conclusion

The proposed governance architecture transforms TechGlobal from an informal, IT-centric governance structure into a mature, business-aligned governance framework. The model establishes clear accountability, executive ownership, Board oversight, and structures governance processes necessary to manage cybersecurity risks effectively.
