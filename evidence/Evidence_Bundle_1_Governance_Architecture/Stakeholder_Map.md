# Stakeholder Map
 
## Purpose
 
This stakeholder map identifies the key governance participants involved in TechGlobal's proposed security governance framework. The map illustrates the relationship between strategic oversight, executive leadership, governance management, and operational risk ownership.
 
---
 
# Strategic Governance Stakeholders
 
## Board of Directors
 
### Responsibilities
 
- Approves cybersecurity risk appetite.
- Provides oversight of enterprise cyber risk.
- Reviews strategic cybersecurity reporting.
- Monitors governance effectiveness.
- Holds executive leadership accountable.
 
### Interest Level
 
High
 
### Influence Level
 
High
 
---
 
## Board Risk and Audit Committee
 
### Responsibilities
 
- Reviews material cyber risks.
- Monitors risk treatment plans.
- Oversees audit findings.
- Escalates significant concerns to the Board.
 
### Interest Level
 
High
 
### Influence Level
 
High
 
---
 
# Executive Leadership Stakeholders
 
## Chief Executive Officer (CEO)
 
### Responsibilities
 
- Provides executive sponsorship.
- Ensures cybersecurity aligns with business objectives.
- Approves major governance decisions.
- Receives escalated cyber-risk reports.
 
### Interest Level
 
High
 
### Influence Level
 
High
 
---
 
## Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
 
### Responsibilities
 
- Leads cybersecurity governance.
- Develops security strategy.
- Oversees security policies.
- Coordinates governance committees.
- Reports cyber-risk exposure.
 
### Interest Level
 
High
 
### Influence Level
 
High
 
---
 
## Chief Risk Officer (CRO)
 
### Responsibilities
 
- Maintains enterprise risk management framework.
- Integrates cyber risk into business risk management.
- Assesses risk treatment effectiveness.
- Supports escalation decisions.
 
### Interest Level
 
High
 
### Influence Level
 
High

# Conclusion

The proposed stakeholder structure distributes governance responsibilities across strategic, executive, operational, and business stakeholders. This approach improves accountability, strengthens risk ownership, and ensures cybersecurity governance supports enterprise-wide business objectives rather than remaining solely an IT responsibility.
