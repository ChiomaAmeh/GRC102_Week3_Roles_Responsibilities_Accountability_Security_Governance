# Conflict Resolution Analysis
 
## Purpose
Security governance decisions frequently require balancing business objectives, risk management, compliance obligations, and operational realities. This document outlines common governance conflicts and recommended resolution mechanisms.

---
 
## Conflict 1: Security Versus Business Objectives
### Example
A business unit wishes to launch a product quickly, while the CISO recommends delaying deployment until security testing is completed.
 
### Risk
Introduction of unmitigated security vulnerabilities.
 
### Resolution
Escalate to the Security Governance Committee for risk-based decision-making.

---
 
## Conflict 2: Security Investment Versus Cost Control
### Example
Security requests additional funding for monitoring tools while Finance seeks budget reductions.
 
### Risk
Underinvestment in critical security capabilities.
 
### Resolution
Use risk-based business cases and executive review.

---
 
## Conflict 3: Legal Requirements Versus Operational Efficiency
### Example
Legal requires stronger privacy controls that business units view as disruptive.
 
### Risk
Regulatory violations and legal exposure.
 
### Resolution
Joint review involving Legal, CISO, and affected business stakeholders.

---
 
## Conflict 4: IT Priorities Versus Security Requirements
### Example
IT prefers system availability while Security requires patching and maintenance windows.
 
### Risk
Delayed remediation of vulnerabilities.
 
### Resolution
Security Governance Committee determines acceptable risk treatment.

---
 
## Conflict 5: Risk Acceptance Authority
### Example
Technical teams wish to accept risks associated with unsupported systems.
 
### Risk
Unauthorized acceptance of enterprise risk.
 
### Resolution
Only designated executive management may formally accept cyber risks.

---
 
## Governance Escalation Principle
Where conflicts cannot be resolved through normal management channels:
1. Security Governance Committee review.
2. Executive Leadership review.
3. Board Risk and Audit Committee escalation if material risk exists.
 
---
 
## Conclusion
Formal conflict-resolution mechanisms ensure governance decisions remain transparent, risk-informed, and aligned with business objectives while preventing disagreements from delaying critical security decisions.
