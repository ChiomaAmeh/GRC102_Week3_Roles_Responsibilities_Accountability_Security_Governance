# Escalation Threshold Framework
## Purpose
This framework defines the conditions under which cybersecurity risks and incidents must be escalated to Operational Management, Executive Leadership, or the Board Risk and Audit Committee.

---
 
## Escalation Threshold Table
| Escalation Level | Criteria | Escalate To |
|-----------------|----------|-------------|
| Operational | Limited business impact, localized issue, no regulatory concern | IT Director |
| Executive | Significant business disruption, financial impact, compliance concerns | CEO and Executive Leadership Team |
| Board-Level | Material business impact, major breach, legal exposure, reputational damage | Board Risk and Audit Committee |
 
---

# Escalation Levels
## Level 1 - Operational
Owner:
IT/Security Operations
Activities:
- Contain the issue.
- Investigate cause.
- Implement remediation.
- Record actions.
Target Response:
Immediate to less than 4 hours.
 
---
 
## Level 2 - Executive
Owner:
CISO and Executive Security Council
Activities:
- Assess business impact.
- Allocate response resources.
- Review external communications.
- Determine escalation requirements.
Target Response:
Less than 24 hours.
 
---
 
## Level 3 - Material/Board
Owner:
CEO and Board Risk & Audit Committee
Activities:
- Review disclosure obligations.
- Assess Board-level risk appetite impact.
- Direct crisis response activities.
- Approve major resource commitments.
Target Response:
48 to 72 hours.
---

## Operational Escalation
### Example Scenarios
- Localized phishing incident
- Single system compromise
- Minor malware infection
- Limited service interruption
 
### Characteristics
- One department affected
- Minimal financial impact
- No regulatory reporting required
 
---
 
## Executive Escalation
### Example Scenarios
- Multiple departments affected
- Exposure of sensitive information
- Significant operational disruption
- Regulatory compliance concern
 
### Characteristics 
- Executive management involvement required
- Increased business impact
- Potential financial consequences
 
---
 
## Board-Level Escalation
### Example Scenarios
- Major data breach
- Enterprise-wide ransomware attack
- Regulatory notification required
- Significant reputational damage
- Material financial loss
 
### Characteristics
- Strategic business impact
- Potential legal exposure
- Board oversight required
 
---
 
## Guiding Principle
Cyber risks should be escalated according to business impact and organizational risk exposure rather than technical severity alone.
 
The objective is to ensure appropriate visibility, oversight, and decision-making at every level of governance.
